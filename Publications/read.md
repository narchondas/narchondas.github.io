# Exploring	an AI-driven	solution	for	analysing consultation results in the European Commission

DG.O 2026 Proceedings - 27th Annual International Conference on Digital Government Research June 01-04, 2026, Omaha, Nebraska, U.S.A., forthcoming 2026


## Abstract

The European Commission, as the executive branch of the European Union, operates within a well-established Better Regulation framework that underpins EU policymaking across the entire policy cycle through evaluation, impact assessment, and stakeholder consultation. The Have Your Say (HYS) portal functions as a single-entry point for citizen engagement. However, the growing volume, multilingual nature, and heterogeneous formats of stakeholder contributions, ranging from short comments to detailed position papers, pose significant analytical and resource challenges for Directorates General. 

Although this research primarily examines the Commission’s public consultations and feedback mechanisms, its findings and the proposed AI-driven solution are equally relevant to public administrations in western liberal democracies at national, regional, and municipal levels that seek to systematically analyse and integrate stakeholder input into policymaking processes. From a practical perspective, the study addresses a critical operational gap, namely the absence of a systematic AI-driven approach capable of processing and analysing large scale, multilingual consultation data. 

From an academic perspective, it responds to a research gap arising from the limited availability of AI system designs tailored to consultation analysis in public governance and the scarcity of scholarly work at the intersection of artificial intelligence, stakeholder engagement, and policy analysis. The study is guided by two central research questions. First, to what extent can AI-enabled tools support policymakers in processing consultation results more efficiently, effectively, and reliably. Second, what institutional, methodological, technical, operational, and usability requirements condition their adoption. Methodologically, the research leads to a qualitative use case focused on the European Commission, structured around the CRISP-DM process model and informed by documentary analysis and expert interviews. 

The main output is a conceptual service design and requirements specification for an AI-driven consultation analysis tool that integrates sentiment analysis, summarisation, clustering, and multilingual processing. Academically, the study contributes by operationalising Large Language Models capabilities within governance contexts. Practically, it offers a model with the potential to reduce analysis time and costs, enhance evidence-based policymaking, and strengthen transparency and trust. The scope is explicitly limited to the European Commission, with emphasis on requirement-driven service design rather than full scale deployment.

**Keywords:** stakeholder consultation, AI-driven solution, natural language processing, large language models, European Commission.

# Trends and patterns of EU AI governance: A text-mining-supported analysis

Punto y Coma, special issue on AI innovation ecosystems, forthcoming 2026.

## Overview

The article examines the evolution of European Union artificial intelligence governance across three regulatory phases, from the early period of coordination and soft law, through the adoption of binding regulation, and into the subsequent stage of implementation and simplification. Drawing on documentary analysis of eighteen EU instruments, a text-mining artefact developed for the purpose, and interviews with four experts, it identifies the trends and patterns characterising this trajectory and considers the trade-offs arising between innovation, competitiveness and societal protection.

The project develops a reproducible NLP-based artefact that analyses selected legislative, policy and strategic documents and maps them across two axes:

- **X-axis:** rights- and citizen-oriented safeguards ↔ innovation
- **Y-axis:** voluntary/soft-law ↔ mandatory/control

The artefact uses dictionary-based NLP techniques, text extraction from PDF documents, tokenisation, lemmatisation, predefined analytical vocabularies and document-level legal-force multipliers.

## Abstract

This article examines the evolution of European Union (EU) artificial intelligence (AI) governance between 2018 and 2026 across three regulatory phases, namely coordination and soft law, binding regulation and implementation and simplification. The analysis combines documentary analysis of legislative, policy and strategic instruments with secondary literature,  a NLP-based text mining artefact that classifies each instrument along an innovation/citizens' safeguards dimension and a mandatory/voluntary dimension, and four expert interviews, with the three sources of evidence triangulated. 

The findings characterise EU AI governance as a dual track model, in which a small core of binding instruments, centred on the AI Act and its amending Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, concentrates regulatory force on citizen rights and safeguards, while an extensive voluntary layer of strategies, guidelines and codes carries both protective guidance and the entirety of the innovation agenda. Notably, no instrument occupies the mandatory innovation quadrant, and regulatory force and innovation orientation are strongly and inversely correlated across the corpus. 

The discussion examines the resulting trade-offs between innovation, competitiveness and societal protection, the pursuit of digital sovereignty through voluntary instruments, and the prospects for a Brussels effect, which is assessed as probable but contingent rather than automatic, given EU technological position relative to the United States and China. The article concludes with recommendations and implications for jurisdictions observing the EU model, including those of Latin America and the Caribbean.

## Artefact

The artefact is implemented as a reproducible Python/Google Colab notebook. It processes 18 EU AI governance documents and generates:

- regulatory spectrum scatter plot;
- four-component vocabulary profile;
- heatmap of component intensity;
- quadrant distribution chart;
- EU analytical word clouds;
- top driver terms by jurisdiction;
- vocabulary audit tables;
- reproducible Excel and HTML outputs.

## Final modelling logic

The final version of the artefact is **v1.3**.

No word-level weights are applied. All vocabulary terms and phrases are counted equally. The only weighting mechanism is the document-level legal-force multiplier applied to the mandatory/control count.
